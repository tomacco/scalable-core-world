Field notes

The turbines on the hill face the planetary wind, every one of them, like sunflowers that decided the sun was overrated. The offshore three stand in the swell on yellow monopiles — the paint is not decoration, it is so the birds see them, and the birds here ride great circles all day.

People ask why the cables sag. Cables are supposed to sag! A taut cable is a worried cable. The sag is where the slack lives, and the slack is what forgives the wind, the cold, and the occasional over-curious butterfly.

At dusk I climb the telescope platform and watch the beacons come on — every turbine blinking together, one red heartbeat for the whole planet. Then the substation lamp flickers on behind me and the night shift begins.

Gridkeeper's rules

One: the grid is a promise, not a machine.
Two: respect the sag.
Three: if the lights are on in every little house, you did your job — nobody claps, and that is how you know it worked.
